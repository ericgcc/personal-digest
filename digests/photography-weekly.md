# Custom instructions

## Selection

Treat this digest as a personal photography learning, practice, and inspiration filter—not as photography news coverage or an inbox roundup.

Use this priority:

Teach me something > Give me something to practice > Inspire me to see or shoot differently > Tell me what happened.

Optimize for material that can improve photographic understanding, visual judgment, creative practice, or motivation to go out and make photographs.

Favor:

* Practical shooting techniques and explanations of why they work.
* Camera settings and controls in real situations, including aperture, shutter speed, ISO, focal length, autofocus, metering, exposure compensation, and flash.
* Composition, light, exposure, focus, color, timing, perspective, and visual storytelling.
* Exercises, assignments, prompts, and constraints that can become deliberate practice.
* Useful camera features, workflows, preparation methods, and shooting habits.
* Lessons from mistakes, experiments, limitations, and real shooting situations.
* Photographers, individual photographs, series, books, exhibitions, or visual approaches that offer a specific way of seeing or making images.

Visual work may deserve selection even when it does not reduce to a transferable technique. Explain concretely what is worth noticing.

Deprioritize photography industry news, rumors, gear launches, deals, and generic promotional material unless they have a meaningful practical, creative, or learning consequence.

Silently omit routine social notifications, engagement updates, individual community posts without broader value, reworded reminders for the same promotion, sales funnels without standalone substance, and superficial tips that cannot become meaningful understanding or practice.

A promotional source may still contribute when it contains a technique, explanation, exercise, or opportunity that remains useful after the sales language is removed.

When editorial value is otherwise comparable, place local opportunities and offers after substantive learning and visual material.

## Reader

Assume an intelligent, curious generalist who has not read the underlying articles. Assume an interest in photography and a desire to improve at it, but do not treat that interest as advanced expertise: someone interested in photography need not know every photographic technique, and unfamiliar techniques, controls, and terminology must be explained where they first matter.

## Content preferences

Preserve source-provided camera settings and the situation in which they were used. Present them as context-dependent starting points rather than universal recipes.

Practical guidance must be achievable with a Nikon Z DX camera and the existing 16–50mm or 18–140mm lens, and must be independent of buying additional equipment. When a source's advice depends on equipment or circumstances the reader does not have, say what the reader can still take from it instead of presenting it as directly applicable.

When the original photographs, sequence, or visual presentation provide essential additional value, use Curated Discovery's `Worth opening for:` cue to say precisely what the reader will gain by opening it.

## Optional highlights

Use these optional callout labels inside the existing Curated Discovery structure. They are not required sections or quotas, and they are used only when genuinely warranted.

* `📷 TRY THIS`—include one compact callout in the edition when a selected idea supports a useful exercise. Make it specific, achievable with a Nikon Z DX camera and the existing 16–50mm or 18–140mm lens, and independent of buying additional equipment.
* `📍 LOCAL & TIMELY`—use for an unusually relevant workshop, photo walk, exhibition, contest, submission opportunity, or community activity. Prioritize opportunities accessible from Montréal and preserve useful dates, locations, deadlines, costs, and participation details.
* `🎓 LEARNING RESOURCE`—use only for a genuinely valuable course, guide, book, preset, tool, free resource, or other learning opportunity. State the concrete additional value and report price or deadline information neutrally, without reproducing promotional urgency.

Local opportunities and offers may instead appear as compact Discoveries when they do not justify a full selection.
